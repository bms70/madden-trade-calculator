Madden League Trade Calculator

How to Use the Calculator

Use this guide to enter a trade correctly, understand the values on screen, and interpret the final verdict.

Release v1.1.0 · Ruleset v4.0

CalculatorHow to Use
Start here: Enter what each team receives. Complete the required information for each player or select the appropriate draft pick, then use Adjusted Value, the verdict, and Trade Intelligence to understand the deal.

1. Build the trade

Put every asset on the side of the team receiving it.

Example: Team A trades a quarterback to Team B for two draft picks.

Team A Receives
• Draft Pick #1
• Draft Pick #2

Team B Receives
• Quarterback
Easy rule: Read each side as “What does this team get?”

2. Add a player

Select Player as the Asset Type.

Required information

  • OVR
  • Age
  • Position
  • Dev Trait

A player does not contribute value until those required fields are complete.

Additional information

  • Cap Hit
  • Years Remaining
  • Years in League
  • Current Role
  • Projected Role
Rookies: Enter 0 for Years in League to apply Year 0 rookie treatment. Leaving the field blank is not the same as entering 0.

3. Add a draft pick

Select Draft Pick, then choose the exact current pick or the appropriate future-pick projection.

Future picks lose value as the pick moves farther away. If timing is blank, the calculator treats the pick as Current draft.

4. Understand the values

Individual Value

The calculated value of one player or draft pick.

Raw Value

The total value of all assets received before package-quality adjustments.

Adjusted Value

The package value after quantity-for-quality protection is applied.

What to watch

Raw Value can look balanced while Adjusted Value shows that one side is receiving the stronger package.

Tip: When judging the final trade, pay close attention to Adjusted Value rather than relying only on Raw Value.

5. Why more assets do not always fix a trade

The calculator gives declining credit to additional assets in a package:

1st asset
100%
2nd asset
90%
3rd asset
75%
4th asset
60%
5th asset and beyond
50%

This protects against quantity automatically equaling quality. Several smaller assets can have a large Raw Value while producing less Adjusted Value against one premium asset.

6. Premium players

Some players require additional return because of their quality and scarcity.

Elite QB

Young, highly rated Superstar or X-Factor quarterbacks receive the strongest player-acquisition protection.

Franchise Cornerstone

Young, highly rated Superstar or X-Factor players can qualify for Franchise Cornerstone protection.

Other player acquisitions

Players outside those categories still receive the standard player-acquisition treatment.

What this means: Matching a premium player’s displayed value does not necessarily make the trade FAIR. The acquiring team may need to provide additional Adjusted Value.

7. Trading up in the draft

Equal point totals do not automatically make a draft trade FAIR when one team is acquiring the clearly better pick.

Trading for 1.01

The 1.01 receives the strongest draft acquisition protection.

Trading for 1.02 or 1.03

These selections also receive premium draft protection.

Other meaningful trade-ups

Moving from a weaker best pick into a clearly stronger best pick can trigger a smaller trade-up requirement.

Near-lateral moves

Moves between picks of similar quality do not receive the same premium treatment.

Basic idea: Moving several lesser picks into one premium pick is a quality upgrade, not merely an exchange of equal point totals.

8. Understand the FAIR window

For draft-only trades where one team acquires the stronger pick, the trade-down package must reach the required return and remain within the permitted FAIR range above that target.

This means a draft-only trade can miss FAIR by being below the required return or by going beyond the permitted range.

9. Read the verdict

FAIR

The trade satisfies the applicable acquisition protections and trade requirements.

QUESTIONABLE

The trade did not qualify as FAIR, but the adjusted-value gap is relatively close and may warrant commissioner review.

UNFAIR

The adjusted-value gap is significant and the trade generally needs additional value or restructuring.

LEAGUE-BREAKING

The adjusted-value gap is substantial enough to raise competitive-integrity concerns.

10. Understand Trade Intelligence

The verdict tells you what the calculator decided. Trade Intelligence helps explain why.

Primary Read

The major rule or trade characteristic driving the result.

Best Asset

Compares the strongest individual asset received by each team.

Raw Value

Compares value before package-quality adjustments.

Adjusted Value

Compares package value after quantity-for-quality protection.

Draft Capital

Shows which team receives more draft-pick value.

Package Structure

Explains whether quantity-for-quality effects are influencing the trade.

Suggested Correction

Identifies which side needs additional Adjusted Value when the trade is not FAIR.

11. Common trade types

Player for picks

Enter the player on the team receiving the player and every pick on the team receiving the draft capital. Then review Best Asset, Raw Value, Adjusted Value, Draft Capital, and Suggested Correction.

Trading for 1.01

Enter 1.01 on the side moving up and the full trade-down package on the opposite side. Pay close attention to Primary Read, Adjusted Value, and Suggested Correction.

Quantity for quality

If one side receives one premium asset and the other receives several smaller assets, compare Raw Value with Adjusted Value to see whether package-quality protection is affecting the deal.

12. Common mistakes

  • Entering assets on the side sending them instead of the side receiving them.
  • Leaving required player information blank.
  • Leaving Years in League blank when you mean Year 0.
  • Looking only at Raw Value.
  • Assuming more low-value assets automatically create a better package.
  • Assuming equal draft points automatically mean FAIR.
  • Ignoring Trade Intelligence and Suggested Correction.
